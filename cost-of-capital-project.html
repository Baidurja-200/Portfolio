Methodology

How the cost of capital was estimated

The analysis followed a corporate finance structure that separates market risk, borrowing cost, and capital structure weight.

Cost of Equity

Used CAPM with a 6.73% risk-free rate, company beta, and a 7.00% equity risk premium to estimate shareholder return expectations.

Cost of Debt

Estimated borrowing cost using risk-free rate plus company-specific credit spread, then adjusted for tax shield where applicable.

Capital Weights

Used equity value and financial debt to calculate E/V and D/V, then combined the components into WACC.

Company Insights

What the numbers indicated

Lowest Risk

Indus Towers

WACC was estimated at 13.25%. The company was effectively equity-funded with only 0.8% debt weight, making the debt tax shield negligible.

Debt Pressure

Sterlite Technologies

WACC was estimated at 14.8%. Its 19.4% debt weight and loss-making tax position raised the effective cost of financing.

Highest Risk

Tejas Networks

WACC was estimated at 17.04%. High beta and order-cycle volatility increased the cost of equity despite limited debt exposure.

Decision Lens

Investment Attractiveness

Lower WACC improved valuation attractiveness, while higher WACC signaled higher required returns and greater business risk.

Takeaways

Finance interpretation

01

Stable cash flows lowered risk

Indus Towers showed a defensive capital profile due to recurring tower-rental revenue and conservative leverage.

02

Debt is not always cheaper in practice

STL's no-tax-shield position made its debt cost fully visible in WACC, increasing its overall financing burden.

03

Business volatility increased equity expectations

Tejas Networks' revenue concentration and order-cycle swings pushed beta higher, making equity capital the main driver of WACC.
