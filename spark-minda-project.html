Detailed Findings

Bottleneck Insights & Material Flow

Critical Constraint

Porsche Housing RH

T6 Heat Treatment (104 pcs / 12 hrs) operated at 93% capacity utilization with a startup cumulative lead time of 44.71 hours. Batching delay at T5 (8 hrs for 90 pcs) and shot blasting contributed significantly to material accumulation.

Parallel Managed

Phinia 6 Cylinder

OP 10 machining possessed the highest cycle time (700s, 63% utilization), followed by OP 20 (664s) and OP 30 (690s). The availability of parallel machining lines mitigated bottleneck severity, maintaining target daily throughput of 138 units.

High Utilization

BorgWarner EAD HP

Machining stages OP 30/40 (290s) and OP 50 (295s) reached 78%–79% utilization. Lead time analysis indicated waiting times prior to these constrained stages accounted for a major fraction of the total 27.84-hour lead time.

High Utilization

BorgWarner EAD LP

OP 30/40 (273s) and OP 50 (258s) experienced 73% and 69% utilization respectively. A drum-buffer-rope mechanism was established to prevent excess WIP buildup before heat treatment and final assembly.

Methodology

Safety Stock Formula & Theory of Constraints (TOC)

Mathematical model used to determine stagewise buffer stock without overstating steady-state machine load.

Minimum Safety Stock Formula

Minimum Safety Stock = Recovery Time × Safety Stock Factor (1.5) × Hourly Demand Rate

Calculated based on daily demand without adding buffer processing into base load, ensuring recovery times accurately reflect true machine availability.

Recovery Time Assumptions

  • High Utilization (>20%): 2-hour recovery time buffer assigned to protect against machine breakdown.
  • Low Utilization (<20%): 1-hour recovery time assigned as fast operations quickly absorb upstream delays.
  • Batch Operations: One full batch + extra buffer reserved for heat treatment & shot blasting.

Drum-Buffer-Rope (DBR) Model

The bottleneck operation acts as the Drum setting the production pace. Strategic WIP buffers protect the drum from starving, while the Rope signals material release at the raw melting stage to prevent shop-floor congestion.

Recommendations

Proposed Operational Actions

R1

Deploy Drum-Buffer-Rope (DBR) Scheduling

Pace raw material release and gating operations to T6 Heat Treatment bottleneck capacity (Porsche RH) to reduce lead times and control WIP buildup.

R2

Implement Stagewise Safety Stock Buffers

Institutionalize calculated minimum safety stocks (456 units for Porsche RH, 506 for Phinia, 614 for EAD HP, 671 for EAD LP) to guard against machine breakdowns.

R3

Institutionalize OEE Dashboard as SOP

Mandate daily breakdown log entries into the structured Excel tool and conduct weekly executive reviews of recurring machine downtime causes.

R4

Evaluate Parallel Line Capacity Expansion

Monitor high-utilization machining stages (BW EAD LP OP 30/40 and Phinia OP 10) for future parallel line investments as client demand scales.
